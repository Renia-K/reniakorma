The Unseen Hand

Stylometric Analysis of Authorial Influence in Classic Literature

DH Benelux 2025, Amsterdam, Netherlands — 3–6 June 2025

The Unseen Hand is a stylometric and digital humanities project analyzing the authorial influence and stylistic crossover between notable literary pairs: T.S. Eliot & Vivienne Haigh-Wood Eliot, and F. Scott & Zelda Fitzgerald. This project investigates whether computational methods can support or challenge traditional literary assumptions about influence and authorship.

The research draws from curated datasets of each pair’s works, sourced from public digital libraries such as Project Gutenberg. Using tools like Voyant, R's Stylo package, and Zeta analysis, the team examined thematic intersections, stylistic patterns, and authorial signatures across texts.

The project proposes new empirical ground for exploring under-researched literary relationships and applies visualization and stylometric techniques to test hypotheses around influence, collaboration, and individuality in writing styles.

Corpus Highlights

  • Authors: T.S. Eliot, Vivienne Haigh-Wood Eliot, F. Scott Fitzgerald, Zelda Fitzgerald
  • Genres: Poetry, fiction, essays (17th, late 19th & 20th centuries)
  • Methods: Stylo in R, Voyant Tools, Craig’s Zeta, authorship attribution modeling

Selection Criteria

  • Empirical Potential: Texts with unresolved scholarly debates or influence-based assumptions
  • Research Gaps: Author pairs with minimal stylometric research
  • Digital Accessibility: Texts freely available in open-access repositories

Authorship

Olga Rojas Valle, Theodora Stavroula Korma, Ece Demirtas

Presented at DH Benelux 2025 in Amsterdam, this project contributes to authorship attribution scholarship and highlights the power of digital literary analysis in uncovering unseen creative influences.
