Unveiling the Forbidden: A Digital Dive into North America's Censored Literature

A geospatial and stylometric investigation of banned books in the United States

Corpus Overview

This repository contains a dataset of 46 books banned in the United States from the 19th century to the present. The project investigates the historical and cultural contexts of censorship and analyzes suppressed literature through topic modeling, stylometric analysis, and geospatial tools.

Themes such as race, sexuality, gender, and social taboos are explored to uncover societal pressures behind literary suppression. The resulting digital archive serves as a resource for educational, historical, and cultural inquiry.

Corpus Highlights

  • 46 censored works spanning from the 19th century to 2024
  • Themes include abolitionism, LGBTQ+ rights, and racial justice
  • Genres include fiction, memoirs, political essays, and poetry

Analysis Tools

  • Zeta Analysis: Using R to contrast preferred and avoided words across centuries
  • Voyant Tool: Interactive modeling and visualizations like word clouds and term graphs
  • GIS Mapping: ArcGIS used to display the spatial distribution of censorship in the U.S.

Project Outcomes

  • A searchable digital archive of banned books
  • Visualizations illustrating thematic trends and censorship triggers
  • Interactive geospatial maps highlighting regional and historical shifts

Geospatial Storytelling in Art and Heritage

This project extends beyond data visualization to explore how mapping can function as a tool for storytelling within the cultural heritage and arts sector. By tracing the geographic trajectory of book censorship across time, the digital map reveals spatial patterns that traditional research methods often overlook.

Geospatial storytelling bridges disciplines—combining literary studies, digital humanities, and cartography—to narrate histories of suppression and resistance. In doing so, it transforms abstract data into accessible, emotionally resonant experiences, inviting viewers to reflect on how geography shapes culture, policy, and collective memory.

This approach aligns with a broader movement in curatorial and heritage work that uses digital tools to reimagine archives, democratize access, and bring hidden stories to light.

Purpose & Impact

“Unveiling the Forbidden” is designed to raise awareness about literary suppression, inspire dialogue about freedom of expression, and provide researchers and educators with tools to study censorship over time and space.
